Theoretical Foundation: The Event Loop & Framebuffers

Unlike terminal tools that execute lines of code and exit, desktop applications use an Inversion of Control (IoC) layout. The Operating System (OS) runs an active Event Loop. Your code registers itself as a listener, sitting patiently inside this loop waiting for OS notifications like window resizes, keystrokes, or display requests.

To render images, we manage a Framebuffer: a giant, flat, one-dimensional array containing your pixel colors. To position a point on a standard two-dimensional grid, we calculate its index inside this linear array using:

$$I = (Y \times W) + X$$

Where $I$ is the index in your array, $W$ is the width of your window in pixels, and $X$ and $Y$ are your coordinate offsets.

Goal

Step-by-Step Instructions

  • Step 2.1: Include softbuffer = "0.4" in your Cargo.toml.

Exercise 05: High-DPI Adaptation
Step 5: Physical vs. Logical Screens
Turn-in directory: ex05/
Files to turn in: Cargo.toml, src/main.rs
Allowed external crates: winit, softbuffer

Goal

On high-density displays (like macOS Retina screens or modern 4K monitors), physical display resolutions are much higher than logical layout coordinates. If you draw pixel coordinates strictly 1:1, your UI elements will look incredibly tiny on modern screens.

Adapt your engine to query the monitor's scale factor (e.g. $s = 2.0$) and multiply your shape layout coordinates and sizes by this scale factor to keep things looking sharp and correctly sized.

Step-by-Step Instructions

  • Step 5.1: Store a scale_factor: f64 in your state, defaulting to 1.0.
  • Step 5.2: When the window is first resumed, grab its scale factor using window.scale_factor().
  • Step 5.3: Listen for WindowEvent::ScaleFactorChanged inside window_event to catch any updates if the window is dragged to a different monitor.
  • Step 5.4: Adjust your drawing math: calculate your layout coordinate sizes by multiplying your logical dimensions by your scale factor. For example: $$\text{scaled\_x} = \text{floor}(X \times s)$$ $$\text{scaled\_y} = \text{floor}(Y \times s)$$
